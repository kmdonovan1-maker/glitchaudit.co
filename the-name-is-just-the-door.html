THE NAME
IS JUST
THE DOOR.

The most important thing to understand before you read anything else in the Glitch Glossary.

You are about to encounter a library of named programs.

The Blockbuster Syndrome. The Founder's Deaf Spot. The Helicopter Owner. The Cannibal Contract. The Sconce Mistake. The Bottomless Trap.

The names are not the point.

They never were.

The name creates distance. The distance is where the freedom lives. You don't need to label it perfectly. You just need to see it running.

The moment you can watch the machine running — whatever you call it, however imprecisely you label it — that moment of seeing is the glimpse. That's the whole game.

The seminar world spent decades selling people increasingly sophisticated frameworks for naming their patterns. More precise taxonomies. More nuanced categories. More elaborate maps of the territory.

And the machine — clever as it always is — took every new framework and turned it into a new loop. A new way to stay busy without actually seeing anything.

"Was that the Founder's Deaf Spot or the Identity Hostage?"

"I'm not sure I identified that correctly."

"Let me research this more before I act."

The map became the destination. The tool became the obstacle.

That's not what this is.

The names in the Glitch Glossary are doors. Walk through them and leave them behind. If you catch something running in your business and you can't remember what it's called — that's fine. Better than fine.

The moment you noticed it running — that was already the glimpse.

Two words show up on every page of this glossary.

A glitch is the machine running its program. Not a character flaw. Not a failure of intelligence or effort. Just prehistoric hardware doing exactly what it was built to do — in a context it was never designed for.

A glimpse is the moment you step outside it. Brief. Unannounced. Requiring no technique. The moment the noise separates from the signal and something clearer comes through.

You have been having glimpses your whole life. This glossary just helps you recognize them for what they are.

Open any entry. Find the one that makes you slightly uncomfortable — the one where something recognizable stirs before you've finished the first paragraph.

That discomfort is the signal.

That's the glitch introducing itself.

That's enough. It has always been enough.
