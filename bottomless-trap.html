THE BOTTOMLESS
TRAP

The promotion designed to drive volume that destroys the value that made volume worth having. Every promotion is a signal. The question is never whether it drives traffic — but what kind of traffic it drives.

Before You Read
The name is just the door. The seeing is what matters. If you recognize something running in your business and can't name it perfectly — that's fine. Read why →
The Glitch

A craft brewery was running consistent Friday nights.

Loyal crowd. Good energy. Real revenue — the kind built on people who came because they wanted to be there, who paid full price because the experience was worth full price, who came back the following Friday because the previous one had been genuinely good.

Then someone decided to run a bottomless beer special. Twenty dollars. All you can drink.

The logic was straightforward. More people through the door. More volume. More activity. The first few weeks looked exactly like growth.

What the numbers didn't show was what was happening to the room. The regulars stopped coming. The vibe had changed. The new crowd wasn't there for the experience. They were there to get drunk cheaply.

Five times the beer. Less than a third of the previous revenue. And the Friday nights that used to feel like something now just felt loud.

The promotion didn't grow the business. It replaced one customer base with a worse one and called it marketing.

The advisors saw it. The regulars felt it and left without explaining why. The owner kept running the special because the room was full and full rooms feel like success even when the math says otherwise.

This is the Acquisition Blindspot and the Price Basement Glitch running together — discounting the product to attract volume and destroying the value that made the product worth coming to in the first place.

Every promotion is a signal. It goes out to everyone — the customers you want and the ones you don't. The bottomless special sent a very specific signal to a very specific crowd.

And they showed up exactly as advertised.

The question that never got asked before the promotion launched — who are we actually attracting with this offer — is the only question that matters before any discount decision gets made.

Recognized By
  • The promotion that changes who walks through the door
  • The regular who quietly stops coming without ever explaining why
  • Revenue that looks flat or declining despite higher volume or traffic
  • The Friday night that used to feel like something and now just feels loud
  • Discount dependency — customers who only show up when the price drops
  • The business that has trained its audience to wait for the special rather than pay the rate
The Inquiry
Who are we actually attracting with this offer?
Is this the customer we want to build around?
A Note on the Inquiry

The Bottomless Trap survives because full rooms feel like success. The inquiry asks a different question — not is the room full but who is in the room and what does their presence signal about what the business is becoming. The answer to that question is always available before the promotion launches. The machine just needs to be quiet enough to hear it.
