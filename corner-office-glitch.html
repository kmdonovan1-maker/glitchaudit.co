THE CORNER
OFFICE
GLITCH

The executive who has confused their position with their identity so completely that protecting the title, the office, the perception of success has become more important than the actual health of the business.

Before You Read
The name is just the door. The seeing is what matters. If you recognize something running in your business and can't name it perfectly — that's fine. Read why →
The Glitch

The corner office was earned. The title was deserved. The track record is real.

And somewhere in the accumulation of those things — the office, the title, the track record — something shifted. The position stopped being a tool for doing the work and started being the thing being protected.

Decisions that once got made based on what was right for the business now get filtered through a prior question: how will this make me look?

Bad news gets filtered before it reaches the top. The consultant gets brought in not to discover what is actually true — but to confirm what the executive already believes.

The gap opens slowly. What the internal reports say and what the front line employees know become two different stories told in two different languages about the same company.

The people delivering information have learned what kind of information gets a good reception and what kind gets the messenger managed out. So the information that arrives at the top gets curated. Shaped. Made safe.

The executive running the Corner Office Glitch is operating on a map of the business that stopped matching the territory months or years ago — and the machine running the glitch makes it extremely difficult to notice the discrepancy because noticing would require admitting something the status program cannot afford to admit.

Blockbuster didn't lose to Netflix because streaming was inevitable. They lost because the machine running their boardroom couldn't distinguish between protecting the business and protecting the identities of the people who had built their careers on the existing model.

The corner office view became more important than the customer's view.

By the time the data confirmed what the front line had known for years it was already too late to act on it without the kind of dramatic acknowledgment the machine had been working overtime to prevent.

Recognized By
  • Decisions made based on how they will look rather than what they will do
  • The consultant brought in to confirm rather than discover
  • Bad news that disappears between floors before reaching the top
  • The gap between what internal reports say and what front line employees know
  • Strategic responses to last year's competitive landscape rather than this year's
  • The moment the view from the corner office becomes more important than the view from the customer's front door
The Inquiry
Who is the I that needs this title to be right?
Where is the leader when the corner office is empty?
A Note on the Inquiry

The Corner Office Glitch is the hardest to name from inside it because the machine running it has made the protection of the position feel identical to the protection of the business. The inquiry creates a separation between the two. The title and the work are not the same thing. The office and the leadership are not the same thing. That gap — once visible — is where the real decisions become available again.
